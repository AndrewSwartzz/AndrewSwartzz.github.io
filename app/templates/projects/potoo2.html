1. Mission Statement

Potoo makes nature exploration more accessible and engaging by combining location-based discovery, species identification, and game-like progression while encouraging users to observe the natural world rather than remain focused on their screens.

Project Summary

Potoo is a mobile nature companion that helps users discover nearby wildlife and learn about the species they encounter. Before exploring, users can browse a field guide to learn about birds they are likely to see in their area.

During outdoor activities, the app encourages users to put their phones away and pay attention to their surroundings. After an observation, users can record what they remember and work through identification clues about appearance, habitat, behavior, season, and calls before receiving an answer.

Users can build a personal collection of identified species and participate in optional quests and location-based activities that encourage continued learning and exploration.

2. Audience

Potoo is designed for beginning naturalists, students, outdoor enthusiasts, and experienced birdwatchers who want to learn more about the wildlife around them.

Beginners can use the app to learn about unfamiliar species and develop identification skills, while experienced naturalists can discover local hotspots and keep track of their observations. Potoo is intended for people who enjoy spending time outdoors but may not know where to start or how to identify what they encounter.

By combining educational resources with optional challenges and collection features, Potoo aims to make nature exploration approachable for a wide range of ages and experience levels.

4. Comparative Analysis

Potoo draws inspiration from existing nature applications while emphasizing a different experience: guided learning, personal progression, and outdoor observation with minimal screen time.

App 1: Merlin Bird ID

Merlin Bird ID, developed by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, helps users identify birds using photographs, recorded sounds, and guided questions. It also provides bird information and regional species resources.

Strengths

  • Multiple identification methods support different situations.
  • Regional information helps users find likely species.
  • Species profiles support learning beyond identification.

Limitations for Potoo's Goals

  • Primarily focused on bird identification.
  • Quick identification may leave less room for deliberate clue-based learning.
  • Quests, collection progression, and location-based game loops are not its primary focus.

Design Decisions for Potoo

  • Provide accessible identification tools and useful species profiles.
  • Use regional information to help users prepare for outdoor exploration.
  • Offer optional clue-based identification that encourages users to think through their observations.

App 2: iNaturalist

iNaturalist is a nature observation platform where users document organisms, receive identification suggestions, and contribute observations to a community-supported biodiversity database.

Strengths

  • Supports many types of organisms beyond birds.
  • Connects observations with geographic locations.
  • Community identification can support learning and scientific knowledge.

Limitations for Potoo's Goals

  • The range of information may feel overwhelming to beginners.
  • Observation and community workflows can require substantial device interaction.
  • Guided, game-like learning progression is not its primary organizing principle.

Design Decisions for Potoo

  • Use location-aware discovery to help users find nearby wildlife.
  • Keep the interface focused and approachable for beginners.
  • Avoid making social feeds, posting, or constant phone interaction central to the experience.
  • Prioritize observing wildlife first and interacting with the app afterward.

5. Personas, Use Cases & Storyboards

User Personas

Persona 1

The Beginner Naturalist

Name: Jamie Jim

Background: A college student who enjoys walking around local parks but knows little about bird identification.

Goals:

  • Find interesting wildlife.
  • Learn to recognize common birds.
  • Gain confidence outdoors.

Frustrations: Unfamiliar bird names and calls can be confusing, and traditional field guides can be difficult to navigate.

Design implications: Provide approachable species information, nearby discovery, and simple identification clues.

Persona 2

The Experienced Birdwatcher

Name: Jimmy James

Background: An experienced outdoor enthusiast who regularly visits parks and records bird observations.

Goals:

  • Discover productive birding locations.
  • Encounter unfamiliar species.
  • Maintain a record of discoveries.

Frustrations: Repetitive activities and excessive screen time can distract from observing wildlife.

Design implications: Offer location-based discovery, a useful collection, and optional challenges without interrupting outdoor activities.

Persona 3

The Casual Explorer

Name: Gregor Kafka

Background: A student who enjoys outdoor activities with friends but has little prior interest in birdwatching.

Goals:

  • Find fun outdoor activities.
  • Learn something new.
  • Explore without extensive prior knowledge.

Frustrations: Birdwatching can initially feel intimidating or difficult to turn into an engaging activity.

Design implications: Provide short quests, clear goals, and visible progress without requiring extensive technical knowledge.

Use Case 1: Discover a Nearby Birding Location

Primary actor: Jamie, the beginner naturalist.

Goal: Find a nearby location where birds can be observed.

Precondition: Jamie has opened Potoo and can access the Explore screen.

Main Flow

  1. Jamie opens Potoo and selects Explore.
  2. The app displays a map with nearby points of interest.
  3. Jamie selects a hotspot on the map.
  4. The app displays information about the location and potentially observable species.
  5. Jamie chooses to visit the location and observe the surrounding wildlife.

Expected outcome: Jamie discovers a place to explore and has an idea of what to look and listen for.

Use Case 2: Identify and Collect a Bird

Primary actor: Jamie, the beginner naturalist.

Goal: Identify an unfamiliar bird and add it to a personal collection.

Precondition: Jamie has observed a bird outdoors and remembers enough details to attempt identification.

Main Flow

  1. Jamie opens Potoo and begins an observation.
  2. Jamie records what they remember about the bird's appearance, behavior, habitat, or call.
  3. Potoo presents relevant identification clues or questions.
  4. Jamie makes an identification attempt.
  5. Potoo reveals feedback and provides information about the species.
  6. Jamie adds the identified bird to My Collection.
  7. Jamie learns about the species and makes progress toward becoming a more confident naturalist.

Expected outcome: Jamie turns an outdoor observation into a learning opportunity and records a new discovery.

Storyboards

Storyboard 1: From Home to Outdoor Exploration

Associated use case: Discover a Nearby Birding Location.

1. Starting Point

Jamie opens Potoo at home and sees the main navigation options.

2. Discovery

Jamie selects Explore and views nearby hotspots on a map.

3. Decision

Jamie selects a hotspot and reviews the location's details and likely bird species.

4. Real-World Action

Jamie visits the location, puts the phone away, and watches and listens for birds.

The Home and Explore screens allow Jamie to move from opening the app to choosing a real-world destination. Location information helps Jamie prepare for the visit, while the final step reinforces Potoo's goal of encouraging direct observation rather than continuous screen use.

Storyboard 2: From Observation to Identification

Associated use case: Identify and Collect a Bird.

1. Observation

Jamie sees an unfamiliar bird in a park and remembers its colors, behavior, and call.

2. Recall

After observing the bird, Jamie opens Potoo and records the details they remember.

3. Identification

Potoo provides clues and questions that help Jamie narrow down possible species before revealing feedback.

4. Progress

Jamie learns about the identified species and adds it to My Collection.

This workflow turns a real-world encounter into a learning opportunity. The collection provides a record of discoveries and a reason to continue exploring.

Technical Direction

React Native & Expo

Potoo is suited to mobile development because its experience depends on location, observation, identification, and interactions that happen while users are outside. React Native and Expo provide a path toward an iOS and Android interface while supporting components, state, JavaScript, and responsive mobile UI.

Godot & Game Design

Potoo also connects to game development through its collection system, progression, identification challenges, and exploration mechanics. Godot can be used to prototype these systems while exploring object-oriented programming, state management, event-driven programming, debugging, modularity, and software architecture.

These mechanics apply game design techniques outside a conventional game. Progression and feedback can encourage learning and exploration without turning outdoor activities into a screen-based experience.

Project Direction

Potoo is an evolving project concept and prototype. Its proposed features combine nature information, identification challenges, location-based discovery, and game-inspired progression into a single mobile experience.

The project's central design challenge is not simply building an identification app. It is designing technology that knows when to get out of the way. Potoo's most important interaction may ultimately be the moment when the user closes the app, looks into the trees, and starts paying attention.
