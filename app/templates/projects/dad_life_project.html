Building a Real Estate Website for a Local Home Buyer

I designed and developed a custom website for Dad Life Property Solutions, a family-owned home-buying company serving the Lehigh Valley. The project combined full-stack web development, responsive interface design, lead collection, real estate content, and digital media into a production website.

Visit Live Website →

Project Overview

Dad Life Property Solutions needed a website that communicated a straightforward and trustworthy approach to selling a home. Rather than presenting the company as a traditional high-pressure real estate service, the site emphasizes honest conversations, local knowledge, flexibility, and a no-pressure selling process.

The live site currently focuses on homeowners who need to sell quickly, have properties requiring repairs, have inherited a home, or do not want to go through traditional showings and listings. The site guides visitors through a simple process: describe the property, receive an honest offer range, and choose a closing timeline that works for them.

My Role

I worked on the project as both the developer and digital media contributor. My responsibilities included designing and implementing the website, developing its interactive functionality, structuring content, and creating video and marketing material for the client.

Real Client Project

This project gave me experience working from a real client's business goals rather than building a purely academic application. Design and technical decisions had to support communication, trust, lead generation, and the client's actual workflow.

Website Features

Lead Generation

The website is structured around turning visitors into potential homeowner leads. Calls to action direct visitors toward learning what their property could be worth and starting a conversation with the company.

Offer Estimator

A central feature is an offer-estimation experience designed to give homeowners an initial idea of what their property could sell for without requiring them to commit to anything. The live site presents the estimator as free, with no signup and no pressure.

Simple Selling Process

The website explains the process in three steps: tell the company about the property, get an honest offer range, and close on a timeline that works for the homeowner.

Responsive Design

The interface was designed to work across desktop and mobile screens while keeping the most important information and calls to action easy to find.

Technology

The project was built as a custom Flask application, giving me control over the application's backend, templates, forms, and data-handling logic.

Python Flask HTML / CSS Tailwind CSS JavaScript SQLite Flask-Mail

Development Process

1. Understanding the Client

I first focused on understanding the company's target customers and how the business wanted to position itself. The site needed to feel local, approachable, and transparent.

2. Designing the User Experience

I organized the experience around the questions a homeowner is likely to have: Is this company legitimate? Is selling my situation appropriate? What happens next? Can I sell without dealing with the traditional listing process?

3. Building the Application

I implemented the website using Flask and Python, connecting the frontend to backend functionality and creating the site's forms and data-handling systems.

4. Deployment

The application was deployed online so the client has a publicly accessible website rather than a project that only runs locally.

Content & Digital Media

The project extended beyond website development. I also worked on video content explaining the home-selling process and topics relevant to homeowners considering selling their properties.

This included informational content around selling a property as-is and helping communicate when working with a cash buyer may or may not make sense. This connected the technical development work with communication, marketing, and emerging media skills.

Designing for Trust

A major design consideration was trust. Selling a home is a high-stakes decision, so the website avoids overwhelming visitors with technical language or aggressive sales messaging.

Instead, the current site emphasizes that the company is local and family-owned, has no hidden fees, and provides honest advice.

Design Principle

The website should make a homeowner feel like they are starting a conversation with a person, not entering a sales funnel.

What I Learned

This project helped me understand the difference between building a website as a technical exercise and building one for an actual organization. Technical decisions had to support a business goal, while visual and content decisions had to support the client's relationship with potential customers.

I gained experience connecting backend development, frontend design, database management, deployment, content creation, and client communication into one project. It also gave me experience maintaining and iterating on a real deployed application rather than treating the project as finished once the code worked locally.
